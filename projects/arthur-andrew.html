The data behind the redesign
  • 1.17% higher conversion on landing pages vs. product pages
  • 72% preferred the new homepage in preference testing
  • 6 research methods deployed end to end
  • Trust gap identified between ad creative and site

Arthur Andrew Medical is a national supplement brand with a loyal customer base for their enzyme-based products like Neprinol, Serretia, and Syntol. The products had strong word-of-mouth and confident, modern ad creative on social. The website that those ads pointed to looked years behind. The brand customers met on social wasn't the one they met when they clicked through.

The data confirmed it. Users who landed on redesigned landing pages from social ads converted at a 1.17% higher rate than users who landed on product pages from the same ads. Same audience, same source. The only thing that changed was the brand experience that greeted them. Conversions were being left on the table because of a trust gap, and the trust gap was the website itself.

My role as lead UX Designer was to rebuild the e-commerce experience around behavior, not assumption. We deployed six research methods, usability testing, preference testing, heatmapping, FullStory session replay, analytics, and competitive analysis, then walked the client into the first meeting with a fully designed homepage backed by the data, not a concept deck. The work extended into marketing automation, email campaigns, social, print, Amazon listings, and tradeshow materials, but the website was where the conversion math actually moved.

A redesign rebuilt on what users actually did.

FullStory-backed product pages. A homepage organized around how customers actually self-select supplements, by health category, not by product line. Mobile-first templates validated with click maps. And marketing automation aligned to mirror the on-site journey. Because design decisions without data are just expensive guesses.

Product pages rebuilt around behavior
FullStory session replays revealed users were rage-clicking product images expecting them to expand, then pinch-to-zooming the page to inspect ingredient panels and feature callouts. The redesigned product page added tap-to-expand image galleries, callout-rich feature breakdowns, and a sticky add-to-cart bar that solved the false-bottom drop-off pattern surfaced by scroll maps.
A homepage led by health goals
Instead of leading with the product line (Neprinol, Serretia, Syntol, Syntol AMD), the new homepage led with eleven health categories: digestive, immunity, heart, joint, hormone balance, lung and sinus, mood, muscle recovery, beauty, animal health, and yeast support. Visitors could self-select into the goal first, then meet the right product second.
Mobile-first, validated with click maps
Cross-device FullStory analysis showed mobile users encountering elements that worked on desktop but disrupted small-screen flow. Mobile templates were simplified, redundant components were removed, and the redesign was validated post-launch through scroll maps and click maps that confirmed friction points were resolved, not just relocated.

Anchored in real session data, not internal opinion.

Six methods, one hypothesis
Usability testing, preference testing, heatmapping, FullStory session replay, analytics, and competitive analysis ran in parallel. Each one tested the same hypothesis from a different angle: that the brand disconnect between ads and website was costing conversions. FullStory surfaced the specific patterns, rage clicks on product images, dead clicks on icons users assumed were filterable, false bottoms above the add-to-cart, that no analytics dashboard could have flagged on its own.
72% chose the new homepage
In preference testing, 72% of users chose the new homepage over the existing one. That data point gave the client an honest answer to a question they'd been hesitant to ask. From there, the redesign moved fast: tap-to-expand product imagery, sticky add-to-cart, a health-category-led homepage, and mobile templates simplified for thumb-reach. Every move was tied back to a finding from research.
Site, email, and ad creative in one voice
The new visual system extended into email triggers, post-purchase flows, social, print, Amazon, and tradeshow materials. Same hierarchy, same warmth, same call to purchase across every touchpoint, which was exactly the trust signal the previous experience was missing. The combined work produced a 58% lift in conversions over the previous baseline.
