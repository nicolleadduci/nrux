Closet Factory, evolved.

A website redesign for a national custom organization franchise leader, rebuilt around a single insight: people don't purchase custom closets. They want the feeling of being organized based on their unique needs.

UX research surfaced six clear opportunities.

01
User Flow
Establish a structured, strategic page-to-page flow that encourages a journey forward through the site.
02
Scannable Content
Optimize the content on each page to a sustainable, scannable level for users.
03
Local SEO
Prioritize local search and consistent SEO standards across franchisee location pages.
04
Page Hierarchy
Strategize the information hierarchy on each page to align with the customer journey.
05
Navigation
Re-organize the main navigation and sitemap to avoid confusion and encourage self-selection.
06
Best Practices
Implement all accessibility, SEO, and UX best practices and requirements.

A digital experience that wasn't keeping up.

At a glance
  • 85% of users felt overwhelmed
  • “Closet Talk” jargon in nav
  • Mobile journey breaking down
  • 1,224 pages, 280 broken metas

Closet Factory has been a leader in custom home organization for over three decades. Customers trusted the work, while sales teams offered free in-home design consultations as their highest converting moment. The challenge wasn't the business. It was the digital experience users encountered, and the disruptive journey throughout the process.

The website used industry jargon in its navigation that usability testing showed was actively confusing first-time visitors. The hierarchy on key pages didn't reflect how people actually shop for custom organization. Visitors arrived expecting imagery, inspiration, and a quick way to talk to a designer, but were greeted by walls of text instead. 85% of moderated testers said they felt overwhelmed. On mobile, the experience broke down even further, with research showing users rage-clicking the hamburger menu and the 61-locations CTA before reaching the consultation form.

My role as lead UX Designer was to start with a brand refresh that defined the visual system, then carry it through to the new website so booking a free in-home design consultation felt like the natural next step for their customers, not a hurdle. The work also extended to email campaigns, print, social, digital ads, and showroom signage.

A website built around the user's needs.

Restructured information architecture. A mobile-first layout system. Imagery promoted to lead role. And accessibility baked in from wireframe stage. Because compliance isn't a feature you bolt on at QA.

Customer-led information architecture
Reorganized navigation around how visitors actually self-select (closets, garages, pantries) instead of internal “Closet Talk” terminology. Removed three legacy sections drawing under 1% of traffic combined: Look Books, Design Board, and Free Guides. That freed the information architecture for the pages earning 17.5% of all site entries: local franchise pages.
Mobile-first by necessity
Usability testing showed 42% of imagery interactions happening on mobile. Every page was designed for the smallest screen first, then progressively enhanced. Mobile menu, locations CTA, and forms simplified for thumb-reach. Post-launch click maps confirm median time on mobile climbed to 19.5 seconds, with the consultation form among the top click targets.
WCAG 2.1 AA, from wireframe
Color contrast, focus states, keyboard navigation, semantic landmarks, alt-text standards, and reduced-motion support were drafted into the wireframes, not added during QA. Every PII issue flagged in research (form data leaking through URL parameters) was resolved at launch.

Anchored in customer research and usability testing.

Three sections, less than 1% of traffic
Look Books drew 0.03% of traffic. Design Board: 0.55%. Free Guides: 0.1%. Combined, all three legacy sections accounted for under 1% of all visits, while taking up roughly 30% of the navigation real estate. Cutting them gave the information architecture back to the pages people actually came to use.
17.5% of entries, 21.2% of submissions
Local franchise pages already drove 17.5% of all site entries and 21.2% of all form submissions before the redesign. The new information architecture made them the centerpiece, with consistent location-page templates, a clear path to a free in-home design consultation, and aligned local SEO across every market.
42% of imagery clicks happened on mobile
Research showed 42% of imagery interactions happening on mobile, against 26% on desktop. Every page was designed for the smallest screen first, then progressively enhanced.
